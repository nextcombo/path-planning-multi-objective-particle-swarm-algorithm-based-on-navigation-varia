# [Path Planning] Multi-Objective Particle Swarm Algorithm Based on Navigation Variables for Trajectory Planning in Motion-Constrained Unmanned Aerial Vehicles [with MATLAB Code]

Introduction: Path planning is a fundamental issue in the application of UAVs (Unmanned Aerial Vehicles), as it determines the flight path from the starting position to the destination required for the UAV to fulfill its mission [1, 2]. The path should be optimal under certain criteria, such as shortest length or minimum energy consumption. It also needs to meet the constraints imposed by the UAV's kinematic model and safe operation [3]. However, some objectives and constraints may contradict each other, leading to the absence of a single global optimum, thus requiring path planning techniques to balance these requirements to achieve the best possible solution.
In literature, A*, sampling-based algorithms, and Artificial Potential Field (APF) methods are the most popular path planning approaches [4]. A* uses a heuristic algorithm to guide its search to find the shortest path between the start and end points [5, 6]. By maintaining a cost function from the start node to the goal, A* extends the path by adding an edge at each step until reaching the goal. However, A* is limited in scalability due to the discrete nature of its search space, resulting in a rapid increase in the number of units with the size of the space.
The APF method does not discretize the search space but defines it as a potential field formed by surrounding objects [7-9]. Then, the UAV is modeled as a particle moving in the field, attracted by the goal and repelled by obstacles. As a result, the UAV moves along the path towards the goal while avoiding obstacles. However, when the complexity of the environment increases, the generated path is not optimized and degraded.
On the other hand, sampling-based methods use randomization to expand the representation of the path tree until it reaches the target [7, 10]. This ensures finding a path to the target if one exists. For example, the Rapidly-exploring Random Tree (RRT) algorithm randomly samples the search space in a biased way towards large unexplored regions [11]. Over time, the algorithm explores more areas and eventually finds the route to the target. However, the RRT algorithm does not optimize the length of the path during the search process. Its variants, such as RRT* [12], can shorten the path but require more computation.
Recently, optimization techniques inspired by nature have been applied to drone path planning due to their ability to produce optimal solutions [13-15]. These techniques formulate path planning as an optimization problem using cost functions and then employ natural-inspired algorithms to solve it, such as the Firefly Algorithm (FA) [16], Genetic Algorithms (GA) [17, 18], Artificial Bee Colony Algorithm (ABC) [19], Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) [20-23] and Ant Colony Optimization (ACO) [24]. These algorithms treat paths as candidate solutions, then improve them through collective intelligence, which varies depending on the phenomenon relied upon by the algorithm. The DE also uses mutation but combines it with differential evolution. On the other hand, ACO uses pheromone and randomization to guide the search process. Other algorithms, such as ABC, FA, and PSO, utilize social cognitive behaviors of the group to explore the solution space.
In natural-inspired algorithms, PSO is often referred to as an effective method for achieving optimal solutions with high convergence speed. It is less sensitive to initial conditions and can adapt to various environmental structures [25, 26]. PSO acquires these characteristics by balancing individual personal experience and overall group experience in the solution space to find potential regions. Several variants of PSO have been introduced for path planning, such as Discrete PSO (DPSO) [20], Mixed PSO [21], Quantum-Behavior PSO (QPSO) [27], and Sphere-Based PSO (SPSO) [22]. However, most algorithms are single-objective, where they combine harvester objectives into a single cost function through weighted sums. Although this approach is easy to implement, combining multiple objectives leads to the optimality of the cost function not necessarily representing the optimality of individual objectives. In practice, most objectives do not achieve their best values simultaneously. Some even contradict each other, requiring a different approach called multi-objective optimization.
In the direction of multi-objective optimization, several techniques have been introduced for path planning, such as Multi-Objective Firefly Algorithm (MOFA) [28], Multi-Objective Particle Swarm Optimization (MOPSO) [3, 29], and Non-dominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm II (NSGA-II) [30]. These algorithms employ a similar mechanism known as the Dominance Principle to directly solve for Pareto optimal regions. Specifically, NSGA-II uses an encumbrance comparison operator with two attributes: non-dominance rank and encumbrance distance, to propagate solutions within potential regions [31]. MOFA employs search methods from the Firefly algorithm to find non-dominated solutions, followed by a migration process to maintain diversity in the Pareto frontier [32]. In MOPSO, non-dominated solutions are used to guide the exploration of the solution space. Two factors, including position update mechanisms and mutation operators, are included to promote diversity [33]. The advantage of multi-objective optimization is to find the best possible solution, forming a set of non-dominated solutions, known as the Pareto front. However, current algorithms do not incorporate constraints imposed by drone movement during the search process. Since these constraints are essential for generating navigable paths, incorporating them into the algorithm is crucial.
In this study, a novel algorithm named Navigation-Variable-Based Multi-Objective Particle Swarm Optimization (NMOPSO) is proposed to generate aerial vehicles that can fly and Pareto optimal paths. Firstly, a set of objective functions and navigation variables are defined to incorporate the requirements and constraints for optimal UAV operations. The Multi-Objective Particle Swarm Optimization is employed to find a set of non-dominated solutions best suited to the objective functions. A mutation mechanism is introduced to avoid premature convergence and enhance search performance. Four scenarios were created based on different complexity real Digital Elevation Models (DEM) maps to evaluate the performance of the proposed method. Our contributions include: (i) the introduction of a Navigation-Variable-Based NMOPSO algorithm that includes kinematic constraints and multi-objectives to generate Pareto optimal paths for autonomous operation of UAVs; (ii) the introduction of an adaptive mutation operator to improve the performance of the population; (iii) the derivation of a formula inspired by Denavit, which converts navigation variables into Cartesian coordinates using the Hartenberg representation for effective path searching and evaluation; (iv) the use of real UAVs for experiments to confirm the effectiveness of NMOPSO in practical operations.

Conclusion In this work, a new path planning algorithm NMOPSO is introduced to consider the kinematic constraints of UAVs to generate their Pareto optimal paths. The algorithm incorporates mechanisms such as navigation variables, fitness evaluation, and adaptive mutation to better explore the solution space of non-dominated solutions. Comparative results show that NMOPSO outperforms other variants of PSO and state-of-the-art metaheuristic optimization algorithms in most criteria, including path length, safety, and smoothness. Additionally, experimental flights with real UAVs have been conducted to verify the effectiveness of our method for actual UAV operations. The overlap between planned flight paths and actual flight paths confirmed the effectiveness of our method for practical UAV operations.
